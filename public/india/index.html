Overview

Teaching with Roblox Studio is a plugin by Mastertech that brings Roblox Studio into the classroom. Students learn computational thinking, programming and generative AI by rebuilding digital environments, and teachers of different subjects find their own subject in what students build. The plugin records what each student does, so that the teacher can follow the learning of the class.

This handbook is written for those who prepare teachers and decide on the use of the plugin in schools, such as coordinators, teacher educators and partner organisations. It describes where the plugin comes from, its pedagogical design, how it runs in a class, how learning is recorded, and how teachers are trained. A last chapter describes how the plugin could be adapted to the schools of a country, taking the schools of the CBSE network in India as the case. A separate document, the Teacher's guide, is written for the teacher who conducts a class.

Available

The plugin in Roblox Studio, in English, Portuguese and Spanish. The environments of the four levels, with their code. The records of each student.

In development

The subject questions. Those of mathematics in the first environment are set up for a pilot, and the other subjects are being planned. The Learning Analysis Dashboard.

Proposed

A partnership with a local partner, 1M1B, which is being developed. With the expertise of 1M1B, the teacher training programme and the adaptation of the plugin to schools of the CBSE network would be developed together.

Handbook › 01 Origin of the plugin

Mastertech

The company

Mastertech is a Brazilian education company founded by Camila Achutti and Fabio Ribeiro. It designs programmes in artificial intelligence, digital literacy and technology for schools, public education networks and companies, and it builds the digital tools those programmes use.

Work with Roblox

Since March 2026, Mastertech has run the Expedição Roblox in partnership with Roblox. As part of this work, Mastertech develops plugins for Roblox Studio. The plugins belong to Mastertech, and Teaching with Roblox Studio is one of them.

Handbook › 01 Origin of the plugin

The Expedição Roblox

The work with young creators

≈5,000young people have become new creators in Roblox Studio since March 2026, with public partners in Brazil and in Mexico

The Expedição Roblox brings adolescents to Roblox Studio as creators of games and digital experiences. It works with public partners in Brazil and, in a Spanish edition, in Mexico.

Several components work together in the programme. Plugins inside Studio guide the first steps of a creator, and one of them includes an AI assistant that builds a first game from the creator's answers. Video tutorials explain the concepts, a printable pack supports the facilitators who run in-person activities, and an online hub gathers the materials.

An active methodology

The Expedição follows what educators call active methodologies. Creators learn by making a game of their own. The programme gives them a scaffold to start from, such as the first game built with the AI assistant and a guided sequence of first steps, and each creator decides from there what to build, change and publish.

The work is open-ended and organised around projects. There is no single expected result, and each creator gives their own style to the objects, scenarios and rules of the game.

Learning and data in Roblox Studio

Working with young creators, the team observed that creating a game in Studio involves the operations that school curricula describe as computational thinking. Creators customise objects through their properties, build scenarios in parts, write scripts that give objects a behaviour, and test and adjust what they made.

In the same period, Mastertech created its own API to receive data from Studio. The plugins send the choices of each creator, the steps completed and the code produced, and these records allow the work of a creator to be followed over time.

The next step, in schools

After a year of the Expedição, Mastertech had a method, materials and plugins used with young creators, and an API that records their work in Studio. The Expedição took place outside school, with adolescents already drawn to creating. The next step is to take this experience into schools, conducted by teachers.

A new application for the classroom

For this step, Mastertech built a new application, Teaching with Roblox Studio, a plugin designed for the classroom. It brings together the method, the materials and the API developed in the Expedição, and adds what the Expedição did not include, the teacher and the school subjects.

The role of the teacher

Teaching with Roblox Studio was designed to be conducted by teachers. A teacher can connect the experience to the subject they teach, treat what students do as evidence of learning, and share the experience with colleagues in other schools. The teacher also brings the experience to students who would not open Roblox Studio on their own.

First tests with teachers

The first tests of Teaching with Roblox Studio with teachers begin in November 2026 in the Centro Paula Souza network, where technical schools combine secondary education with professional training.

Roblox and Roblox Studio

Roblox as a platform

Roblox is an online platform where people play, create and share experiences built by other users. Tens of millions of people use it every day, many of them adolescents, and the experiences they play are made by a community of creators who work in Roblox Studio.

Roblox Studio

Roblox Studio is the free application Roblox provides for creating those experiences. A creator builds environments with three-dimensional objects, describes them through properties in windows such as the Explorer and the Properties panel, and programs their behaviour in Luau, the scripting language of Roblox.

Studio includes generative AI features, tests an experience in Play mode and publishes it to the platform. Creators extend Studio with plugins, which are distributed through the Creator Store inside the application.

Studio as a teaching platform

Mastertech chose Roblox Studio for classroom use for reasons that can be checked one by one.

In brief

  • The plugin takes that work into school, conducted by teachers and connected to the school subjects, in a free tool that many students already know.

Handbook › 02 Pedagogical design

Teaching with Roblox Studio

The product

Teaching with Roblox Studio is a plugin for Roblox Studio designed for the classroom and conducted by a teacher. Students learn computational thinking, programming and generative AI by rebuilding digital environments in Studio, and the school subjects take part in what they build.

The plugin organises the experience in four levels of increasing complexity, and each level has an environment built for it in Studio. In the environments of all levels, the construction and the code are in place.

An active methodology

Teaching with Roblox Studio follows the active methodologies that the Expedição Roblox used with young creators. Students learn by building. The plugin gives them a scaffold to start from, with a complete environment, a building plan and a line of code for each part, and students make their own decisions on top of it.

The design seeks the engagement of students and their sense that the path belongs to them. The same environment can open different paths, and each student moves through them at their own pace.

Scaffold

A complete environment, a building plan and a working line of code for each part give every student a starting point.

Ownership

Students choose values, change the code beyond the options offered and save their own version of the environment at the end of a level.

Own pace

Each student works on a computer, runs and adjusts the code as many times as needed and moves to the next part when ready.

Environments as examples of a design

The environments are examples of a design. The same design can be applied to places and situations of other contexts, including local ones, and to other possibilities chosen by schools. The examples in this handbook come from the environment of the first level, a riverside village.

The mathematics questions of the first environment are set up for a pilot and will be reviewed by subject specialists. The questions of the other subjects are being planned.

What a class looks like

The teacher opens the plugin, chooses the language, the level and the subject, and selects the parts of the path, the questions and AI mode the class will work with. Students then meet a complete environment, which the plugin takes apart and asks them to rebuild one part at a time.

Each part returns through a line of code. Students predict what the line will do, run it, read it and change it. They then answer a question from the subject the teacher chose, and ask the generative AI of Roblox to change the same part and compare its work with their own code. The plugin records each of these actions.

What students learn

Students learn in three areas during the same work. The first is computational thinking and programming, which is the core of the experience. Students learn to conceive a system in parts and to control it with code.

The second is generative AI. Students use it on the environment they are building, and they learn to direct a model and to judge what it produces. The third is the school curriculum. The environment holds values and situations that a school subject can read, so the teacher of that subject finds their own content in what students build.

Code is the base of the other two areas. The subject reads the state of the environment the student has built, and the AI acts on the same part.

Handbook › 02 Pedagogical design

Computational thinking and programming

Computational thinking and programming are the core of the experience. Students learn them by rebuilding an environment and by reading and changing its code.

Building a complex environment in parts

The experience starts from a complete and plausible environment, with many elements working together. The plugin takes it apart, and the student rebuilds it in a defined order, one part at a time, each part with one programming concept.

In doing so, the student experiences how something complex is conceived in parts, in a sequence and in small increments. Decomposition is the trace of computational thinking that this work leaves. At the end of a level, the student holds the complete code of the environment and can change it.

Prediction and adjustment

Work on each part follows the sequence that Sue Sentance, Jane Waite and Maria Kallia describe as PRIMM. The student predicts the effect of a value, runs the code, reads the line part by part to understand what each part does, and then changes the values beyond the options offered.

Reading and changing working code before writing new code lowers the entry barrier, both for students and for teachers who have not programmed before. Each cycle takes a few minutes and repeats at every part, so students test, observe and adjust many times in a single lesson.

Real code in Luau

Students work with Luau, the language Roblox Studio uses, inside the same tool that professional creators use. Each line they meet is real code that runs in the environment.

The concepts follow a progression. At the first level, an instruction changes one property of one object, through numbers, positions in three axes, lists of fixed values, paths between objects and text.

At the second level, variables name values, the same variable is used in several lines, and the order of instructions changes the result. Loops, conditions and functions belong to the third level, and events and data tables to the fourth.

Handbook › 02 Pedagogical design

Generative AI

Once students have rebuilt a part of the environment with code, they work on the same part with generative AI.

AI acting on the same environment

At the end of each part, Studio enters Play mode and the student writes requests in English to the generative AI of Roblox about the same part of the environment. The AI answers with values, a line of code and a description of the result. Because the AI works on something the student has just built and understood, the student is in a position to judge its output.

Directing and evaluating AI

The student compares vague and precise requests and observes how the precision of a request changes the result. At the first level, the student also meets structured outputs, the instructions that set the behaviour of a model and the setting that makes its answers vary.

At the second level, the student adds context and examples to a request, builds templates that can be reused and identifies answers that a model made up.

In each case the student decides whether to accept, revise or reject what the AI produced, and checks it against the code. These competences correspond to the domains of the AI Literacy Framework published by the OECD and the European Commission.

Handbook › 02 Pedagogical design

The school curriculum

The environment that students have built with code holds values a school subject can read. Mathematics is the first subject set up for a pilot.

School subjects as a matrix

Each part of an environment produces a state that a school subject can read, such as a colour as three numbers, the height of water as a measurement, or the position of an object as coordinates.

A question from the subject attaches to that state. The programming core stays the same, and a teacher enters the experience through the subject they teach. In the first environment, the design covers eight subjects, which are mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, geography, history, language and art. The mathematics questions are set up for a pilot, and those of the other seven subjects are being planned.

Mathematics as the pilot

Mathematics was chosen as the first subject because the code already contains mathematical ideas the teacher can discuss. The examples below come from the first levels.

  • Variables. A variable in code and a variable in algebra share a name and differ in meaning. The line carga = carga + 1 is an instruction to compute the right side with the old value and store the result. The same symbols in an algebra lesson form an equation with no solution.
  • Coordinates. Positions written as three coordinates connect programming to coordinate geometry.
  • Counting. Colours written as three numbers from 0 to 255 lead to counting combinations, 256 × 256 × 256, and to powers of two.
  • Sequences. The order of instructions, and the repetition that a loop later replaces, connect to sequences and iteration.

Each question takes its numbers from what the student has just built. For example, in the third part of the riverside village, the height of the flood water is a value in the code, written in the units of Roblox. The question asks how many metres the water rose, using 1 unit equal to 0.28 m.

Other subjects

The questions of the other seven subjects are being planned. They follow the same design as mathematics, with each question taking its data from the state of the environment.

Handbook › 02 Pedagogical design

Progression across levels

The three areas advance together, and the SOLO taxonomy describes the structure of reasoning at each level.

The SOLO taxonomy

The levels of the experience follow the SOLO taxonomy, proposed by John Biggs and Kevin Collis in 1982. SOLO describes the structure of the reasoning in an answer, independently of its content. It has five levels. The first, prestructural, describes an answer that does not yet grasp the problem. The experience starts at the second level, because students who reach it are expected to have passed the first.

Progression across levels

At the unistructural level, the student handles one aspect of a problem at a time and its effect. At the multistructural level, the student handles several aspects, each still treated on its own. At the relational level, the student links the aspects into a structure and explains how they depend on each other. At the extended abstract level, the student carries that structure into situations not seen before.

Each level raises the complexity of the environment, of the code, of the work with AI and of the subject questions together. The levels connect, so that the repetition written by hand at the second level prepares the loop of the third.

MultistructuralVariables, the same variable in several lines, several properties of an object, a variable that changes value, repetition written by hand and the order of instructions.Context and examples in a request, request templates, made-up answers, answers as lists and the comparison of requests.

A scale across curricula

Mastertech chose a scale based on the structure of reasoning so that the same experience could serve different curricula. In Brazil, curricula differ between states, and other countries organise school years in their own ways. A teacher in any of these systems can use the same scale to describe how a student reasons in code, in the work with AI and in the subject.

The method as a whole

The method combines the elements described in this chapter. The scale sets the levels, each level rebuilds an environment in parts, and each part goes through the same moments, in which code, the school subject and generative AI work on what the student has built.

In brief
  • Students learn computational thinking and programming by rebuilding an environment in parts, use generative AI on the same environment and apply their school subject to what they built.
  • The three areas advance together across four levels of the SOLO taxonomy, a scale that can be set against different curricula.

Handbook › 03 How the plugin works

What the plugin needs to run

Teaching with Roblox Studio runs as a plugin inside Roblox Studio. A class needs computers that run Studio, a Roblox account for each student and the plugin installed. The examples of how the plugin works come from the first level, the riverside village.

Computers and connection

Roblox Studio runs on computers with Windows or macOS. It does not run on Chromebooks or tablets. Each computer needs an internet connection, because students sign in to Studio and AI mode uses the generative AI of Roblox online. The number of computers per class, and whether students work alone or in pairs, depend on each school.

Each student signs in with a Roblox account. The way accounts are created and managed for students depends on the age rules of Roblox and on the policy of each school, including the consent of parents where it applies.

Installing the plugin

The plugin is distributed inside Roblox Studio. The teacher finds it in the Creator Store, through the Toolbox, installs it and manages it in the Plugins tab. Students use the same installation on their computers.

Handbook › 03 How the plugin works

Setting up the class in the plugin

Before the class, the plugin guides the teacher through a sequence of screens that set up the experience. During the class, a settings bar lets the teacher change what students see.

Configuration screens

Before the class, the teacher sets up the experience in the plugin. The teacher chooses the language, and each language follows the way school is organised in a country. The teacher then chooses the profile for setting up a class, sees a screen that explains the SOLO levels, chooses the level and the subject, and selects the parts of the path and the questions the class will work with.

A last screen summarises these choices. There the teacher also enables AI mode, which uses the generative AI of Roblox, in line with the age rules of Roblox and the policy of the school. Students then enter the version the teacher has set up.

When the environment opens, a table pairs each step of the level with its programming concept and with the topic of the chosen subject, so the teacher sees the plan of the level before the class begins.

Class settings bar

During the class, a bar at the bottom of the panel lets the teacher show or hide the subject questions, the explanations of programming concepts and the missions that ask students to work in the windows of Studio. The teacher also chooses whether each step opens with the focus on the code or on the Explorer, the window where students see the objects of the environment.

Handbook › 03 How the plugin works

The structure of a level

Each level of the plugin corresponds to a level of the SOLO scale and has an environment built for it.

The complete environment

A level opens with its environment complete. In the current version, the environment of the first level is a riverside village, with a river, houses on stilts, a pier with canoes, crops and a forest. It is one of the environments created for the plugin, and it can be adapted to other places or replaced by environments of other contexts.

The camera travels over the village, and students can explore it in Studio before any part is removed. Starting from the complete environment gives students a picture of what they are going to build and of how its parts work together.

Taking it apart and the building plan

The plugin then removes parts of the environment, and a building plan shows the order in which they return. Each step brings back one part with one programming concept and one concept of AI, so the plan is also the sequence of what the level teaches.

End of the level

When the last part returns, the plugin shows the complete code of the environment, with each line the student worked on. A manual lists the commands used and the tools of Studio involved. The plugin then changes two or three values in the code and shows their effect, so students see where the code can be changed. A free area, built on a prepared base, lets each student apply what was learned and save their own version of the environment.

Handbook › 03 How the plugin works

The structure of a step

Moments of a step

Each step goes through six moments, which are the same in every step of every level. Repeating the same sequence lets the teacher and the class learn its rhythm once. In the plugin, the six moments are shown on eight screens. The first step of the village, the colour of the river, shows how they work.

The step opens with the part of the environment that will return, its concept and a mission in Studio. The student then makes a decision, choosing among options for the colour of the water. The choice becomes a line of code in Luau, with the changed part marked, and the student runs it.

The river changes colour, and the plugin introduces the concept involved, a property. The student reads the line part by part, and sees that the same change can be made in the Explorer and the Properties window of Studio.

In the modification moment, the student changes the three numbers beyond the options offered and runs the line again. A question from the chosen subject follows. In mathematics, it asks how many colours fit in three numbers from 0 to 255.

AI mode in the step

The last moment of each step is AI mode. Studio enters Play mode, and the student writes a request in English to the generative AI of Roblox about the same part of the environment.

In the first step, the AI answers with three values for the colour of the water, writes the line of code that applies them and explains the line part by part. It also describes the result in words. In the example shown in this handbook, it described the water as clear and transparent near the bank, although the line changes only its colour.

The student compares the answer with the code and with what changed in the environment, and the teacher uses the comparison to discuss with the class what the AI did, what it claimed and how the request could be more precise.

In brief
  • The school needs computers with Windows or macOS, a connection and a Roblox account for each student, and the teacher sets up the class in the plugin.
  • Each level starts from a complete environment rebuilt step by step, and each step goes through the same moments, from the decision of the student to AI mode.

Handbook › 04 Learning records

Student records and the Learning Analysis Dashboard

Each action a student takes in the plugin leaves a record. The records go from Roblox Studio to a tool built by Mastertech, which organises them for the teacher. This way of recording work in Studio was developed during the Expedição Roblox and adapted to the classroom.

What the plugin records

The plugin records the time each student spends on each step and the number of attempts. It also records the choices made and the values the student set beyond the options offered, the answers to the checks for each concept, and the subject questions opened and answered.

In AI mode, it records the requests the student wrote and which of them changed the environment. At the end of each level, it records the changes the student made to the code. Each record relates to a concept of the level, so the records can be read against what the level teaches.

From the plugin to the teacher

The code of the plugin, written in Lua, sends the records of each student to an API built by Mastertech. The Learning Analysis Dashboard, also built by Mastertech, reads the records from this API and organises them for the teacher. It is planned to show the performance of each student, the time spent and the interactions with code, with AI and with the subjects. The tool is in development.

Handbook › 04 Learning records

The teacher's reading of learning

The dashboard

The dashboard is designed to give the teacher two readings of each student. The first estimates, concept by concept, how far the student has mastered what the steps teach, using the evidence defined for each learning outcome.

The second classifies short explanations written by the student according to the SOLO levels, so that the teacher sees how the student reasons about a concept, in addition to the number of correct answers.

In the class view, each cell shows the structure of a student's understanding of a concept, from no evidence yet to aspects related into a whole.

Validation

The reading of the dashboard is tested before it reaches a classroom. Simulated students, AI agents built with known profiles of understanding, go through the activities and produce records and explanations, and the dashboard has to recover those known profiles.

In the classroom, teachers then assess their students without seeing the dashboard, and the two readings are compared. Two teachers also classify the same student explanations by SOLO level, so that the agreement between teachers can be compared with the agreement between teachers and the dashboard.

The teacher's judgement

The teacher's own observation remains the reference for assessing a student. The dashboard adds a record of what each student did in the plugin, which the teacher reads together with their own notes, such as the observation journals that many curricula ask teachers to keep.

In brief
  • The plugin records the choices, attempts, answers, requests and code of each student and sends them through an API built by Mastertech to the Learning Analysis Dashboard.
  • The dashboard is in development, will be tested before it reaches a classroom, and is read together with the teacher's own observation.

Handbook › 05 Teacher training

Competences of the teacher

Teaching with Roblox Studio is conducted by teachers of the school subjects, most of whom have not programmed before. Teacher training prepares them to run the experience with a class, to bring it into the subject they teach and to read what their students understood.

Conducting, adapting and reading

A teacher who completes the training conducts the steps of a level with a class. The teacher asks the questions that lead students to predict, read and compare, and conducts AI mode with criteria for accepting, revising or rejecting what the AI produces.

The teacher also brings the experience into their own subject. They read the state of an environment from the point of view of what they teach, and write a question for an existing step. Mathematics is the reference of the first tests, and the same work applies to physics, geography, language and the other subjects.

Finally, the teacher reads what students understood. They combine their own observation with the records of the plugin and with the explanations students write, and recognise the SOLO level of an explanation.

These competences draw on the AI competency framework for teachers published by UNESCO in 2024. The training aims at its first level, acquire, and takes a first step into its second level, deepen, in the use of AI in teaching.

Handbook › 05 Teacher training

Teacher training programme

Proposed
A first outline of the programme

This outline sets the backbone of the training, its content and its objectives. Each time the plugin is adopted, the programme is developed with a local partner, who brings the knowledge of the teachers and schools of that place.

Content and format

Teachers develop these competences in a training programme designed by Mastertech for subject teachers who have not programmed before. The programme is organised by content, in modules that follow the structure of the experience, from the curriculum and the programming core to AI, the reading of learning and the teacher's own subject.

In each module, teachers first go through the experience as students, in Roblox Studio with the plugin, and then discuss it as teachers. Each module ends with something the teacher produces for their own class.

The programme is developed with a local partner in each country, whose knowledge of teachers and schools shapes the training. In India, this partner is 1M1B, and the partnership is being developed.

The format is adapted to each partner. The number of meetings, the length of each meeting and the use of asynchronous mediation are defined with the partner organisation and the teachers. Mathematics is the reference of the first tests in all modules.

The modules

The curriculum and the whole path

Teachers go through the first level as students and compare it with the goals of computational thinking in their own curriculum.

Objective

To map the programming and mathematics concepts of the level and where they meet the textbook the teacher uses.

Objects and data

Teachers work on how an environment is described as objects with properties, and how a colour, a time or a position becomes a number, in short cycles of prediction and adjustment.

Objective

To modify a step, with the prediction written before running it, and to identify the mathematics it opens.

State and sequence

Teachers move from describing objects to changing them over time, with variables, the order of instructions and repetition, and discuss the variable in code and the variable in algebra.

Objective

To explain a step to students and to write mathematics questions connected to it.

Generative AI in the creative process

Teachers use AI mode with vague and precise requests, and observe how answers vary and how a model can make claims the code does not support.

Objective

To set criteria for accepting, revising or rejecting an AI output in class.

Reading learning and the teacher's own subject

Teachers read student explanations by SOLO level, using their notes and the records of the plugin, and plan how the experience enters their subject and timetable.

Objective

To write a question from their own subject for an existing step and plan the first lessons with a class.

Training trainers

The programme is designed so that a first group of trainers can conduct it with further groups of teachers.

In brief
  • Teacher training prepares subject teachers to conduct the experience, bring it into their subject and read what students understood.
  • The training is organised by content and objectives, and its format is adapted to each partner.

Convergence with the CBSE curriculum

A reading from outside

This chapter is our reading of the CBSE curriculum, built from documents that are publicly available. The knowledge of 1M1B about the schools of this network and their teachers is what will make an adaptation right.

Mastertech has studied the curriculum of CBSE on computational thinking and artificial intelligence and the documents that accompany it. What follows is a starting point for the conversation with 1M1B, to be corrected and completed with its expertise.

1M1B, an organisation that works with young people, schools and teachers across India, saw Teaching with Roblox Studio and recognised in it a possible response to a current need of the schools affiliated to CBSE.

Computational thinking and artificial intelligence have entered the CBSE curriculum, and subject teachers are expected to teach them. 1M1B observed that computational thinking tends to reach classrooms as theory, with few occasions for students to apply it.

The plugin is a product of Mastertech, and its use in CBSE schools would involve adaptations to their curricula, classes and conditions.

Curriculum

In the CBSE curriculum

CBSE has introduced a curriculum of computational thinking and artificial intelligence in the school years, with the intention that students practise these skills regularly and that subject teachers take part in teaching them.

In the plugin

The skills the CBSE curriculum names, such as decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction and algorithmic thinking, are the reference for the outcomes of computational thinking. The work stays within mathematics, next to the textbook that the CBSE resource book follows.

Teaching and assessment

In the CBSE curriculum

In the CBSE curriculum, the resource book of computational thinking follows the mathematics textbook. Subject teachers teach computational thinking, and assessment relies on projects, practical work, reflections and the observation of the teacher. The curriculum does not prescribe a platform, and schools choose where projects take place.

In the plugin

Mathematics is the first subject, and the alignment of its questions with the NCERT chapters is part of the adaptation. The training is written for subject teachers who have not programmed before. The records of the plugin and the written explanations of students are designed to be read with the teacher's observation. The experience runs in Roblox Studio, which is free to use.

Teacher development

In the CBSE curriculum

The National Education Policy 2020 expects teachers to take part in continuous professional development every year, and CBSE chose computational thinking and artificial intelligence as the training theme for teachers in the 2026–27 school year.

In the plugin

The teacher training programme works on this theme, and its format can fit within the professional development of each school.

Learning outcomes for CBSE schools

Form of the outcomes

The learning outcomes describe what a student understands at the end of each level, in the form CBSE uses. They are grouped in the three areas of the experience, which are computational thinking and programming, generative AI, and the school subject. Each outcome is linked to a reference in the CBSE curriculum or in the AI Literacy Framework of the OECD, and names the evidence the plugin records to verify it.

The outcomes of computational thinking and programming are written for the four levels. The outcomes of AI and of mathematics are written for the first two levels, and those of the other levels will follow the AI concepts and the subject questions of each environment. All outcomes are a proposal, to be reviewed with subject specialists and tested in a pilot before they are used to assess students.

About this guide

A first draft

This guide is a first draft, written by Mastertech from the current version of the plugin. It will be developed, reviewed and approved together with 1M1B before it reaches teachers.

1M1B brings the knowledge of the teachers and schools who will use the guide. What follows is a starting point for that work, and its content, its examples and its language may change.

This guide is for the teacher who will run Teaching with Roblox Studio with a class. It goes from the installation of the plugin to the end of a level, with the screens of the plugin and the questions the teacher can ask at each moment.

The handbook explains why the experience is organised as it is, with its pedagogical design, the records of learning and the teacher training programme. This guide explains how to carry it out in a lesson. The riverside village of level 1 is the example used throughout, and the way of conducting a step applies to other environments built on the same design.

G1

Before the first class

The experience runs inside Roblox Studio, so the computers of the class need to run Studio, and each computer needs the plugin installed.

Computers

Roblox Studio is a free application for Windows and Mac. Roblox does not list Studio for Chromebooks or tablets, so the experience needs desktop or laptop computers.

The panel of the plugin and the 3D window share the screen, so the recommended resolution makes a real difference in class. Each computer also needs an internet connection, because Studio requires signing in with a Roblox account and the AI mode uses the Roblox generative AI. The rules for student accounts and the consent of parents follow the age rules of Roblox and the policy of the school, as described in chapter 03 of the handbook, in “What the plugin needs to run”.

Installing the plugin

The plugin is distributed inside Roblox Studio, through the Creator Store, and is installed once on each computer.

  1. Step 1
    Open the ToolboxIn Roblox Studio, signed in with a Roblox account, open the Toolbox and choose the Creator Store.
  2. Step 2
    Find the plugin pageFilter the Creator Store by Plugins and search for the name of the plugin. Open the plugin page. The name of the plugin in the Creator Store will be given when it is published.
  3. Step 3
    InstallOn the plugin page, select Install. The plugin is added to the Plugins tab of Studio.
  4. Step 4
    Check the installationThe Manage Plugins button, in the Plugins tab, lists the installed plugins and lets the teacher enable, disable, update or uninstall each one.
  5. Step 5
    Leave the left column to the pluginThe plugin conducts the experience in a panel on the left of Studio. If other plugins open panels in that column, close them or disable them in Manage Plugins, so the panel has the whole column.

The screen of Studio with the plugin

Once the plugin is open, the screen of Studio has six areas that the teacher and the students use during the class.

Moving in Studio

Students who have never used Studio need a few minutes with the camera before the first step. The Studio tips box shows the controls during the whole class.

Preparing the teacher's computer

Before the first class, the teacher can run level 1 alone on the computer that will be projected, choosing the subject of the class, so that each screen is familiar when the students reach it. The modules of the teacher training programme, in chapter 05 of the handbook, follow the same practice.

In brief
  • Each computer needs Roblox Studio, an internet connection and the plugin installed from the Creator Store.
  • The teacher runs level 1 alone before the first class, on the computer that will be projected.
G2

Setting up the class

The configuration takes five screens and is made once by the teacher. Students who open the plugin with the student profile go straight into the experience with the teacher's settings.

  1. Step 1
    LanguageThe language of the class. Each language follows the way school is organised in a country.
  2. Step 2
    ProfileTeacher, to configure the class. Students use the student profile.
  3. Step 3
    Level of understandingLevel 1 for a class that starts the experience. Before the choice, the plugin explains the SOLO levels.
  4. Step 4
    SubjectThe subject of the questions attached to the steps, with its topics listed before the choice. In the current version, mathematics has a question in every step of level 1.
  5. Step 5
    CheckA summary of level, subject and parts of the path, including AI mode, which the teacher enables here. Each choice has a Change button, and a last button confirms and starts.

The class settings bar

The bar at the bottom of the 3D window stays available during the whole class. Turning a part off shortens the class without changing the order of the steps, which helps when a lesson has less time than planned.

  • Subject questions turn the question of the chosen subject on or off in each step.
  • Programming concepts turn the explanation of the concept of each step on or off.
  • Missions in Studio turn on or off the tasks that ask the student to make the same change with the Explorer and Properties of Studio, without code.
  • Focus sets whether the work starts from the code or from the Explorer.

The opening of the level

After the setup, the plugin presents the path of the level. The complete village is built and shown with the camera touring it, the village is taken apart into six parts, and a building plan shows the six steps that bring each part back with a line of code.

Restarting a level

To restart a level with another class, use the Start button at the bottom of the panel and choose the level again. Start clears the environment in the open place, and opening the level rebuilds it complete, ready for the first step.

In brief
  • The teacher configures the class once, in five screens, and adjusts the class settings bar during the lesson.
  • The level opens with the complete environment, its decomposition and the building plan.
G3

Conducting a step

Every step of a level follows the same sequence of moments. The class learns the sequence in the first step and repeats it in the others, which leaves the attention of the students on the concept of each step.

Organising the class

  • With one computer per student, each student follows the steps at their own pace, and the teacher brings the class together at the subject question and at the AI mode.
  • With one computer per pair, the two students can take turns at the keyboard from one step to the next, and both answer the subject question.
  • Doing the first step together, with the teacher's computer projected, lets the class learn the sequence of moments once before working alone.
In brief
  • Each step runs through the same moments, shown on eight screens, and the class learns the sequence in the first step.
  • At each moment, the teacher asks students to predict, read or compare.
G4

Closing a level

At the end of a level, the plugin gives the student the complete code of the environment and a place to work with it.

The end of the level is the moment for students to explain, in writing, what one line of the complete code does and what would happen if a value changed. These short explanations are the material that the Learning Analysis Dashboard is designed to classify by SOLO level, as described in chapter 04 of the handbook.

Records kept by the teacher

Observations in class

Notes in the teacher's own observation journal, such as the Teacher Observation Journal that CBSE proposes, on which students predicted before running a line, which changed values beyond the options, which checked an AI answer against the code, and where the class hesitated.

Students' written explanations

The sentences written at the end of the level about what a line of code does and what would happen if a value changed. Kept by student and by level, they show how each student's understanding changes from one level to the next.

In brief
  • The end of the level gives students the complete code and asks them to explain a line in writing.
  • The teacher keeps observations in class and the students' written explanations.
G5

Ongoing development

The plugin is developed in versions, and each version is revised with what teachers and students show in class. These are the improvements under way, and what each one changes in the teacher's work.

An introduction to Studio

A short introduction to the windows, menus and vocabulary of Studio, before the first step, so that the first lesson can concentrate on the first concept.

Subject questions of the other levels

The environments of the four levels are built in Studio. The mathematics questions of the first level are set up for a pilot, and the questions of the other subjects and levels are being planned, so that a class can follow each level with a question in each step.

Learning outcomes for each class

A version of the levels and of the learning outcomes set against the school years of each country where the plugin is used, built with local partners.

A reading of the class for the teacher

The Learning Analysis Dashboard, described in chapter 04 of the handbook, which bring together the records of each student. Until it is available, the teacher's observations and the students' explanations are the record of each class.
